Exploring the Himalayas

A journey through silence, snow, and self-discovery

The First Call

The Himalayas have always called to those who seek something beyond the ordinary — a place where the air is thin, the silence is loud, and every step feels like a conversation with the earth itself. Most people hear this call once. Some are lucky enough to answer it.

I remember the morning I first saw the peaks of Manali draped in fresh snow. The bus had stopped at a small dhaba, steam rising from clay cups of chai, and there it was — the mountain, just sitting there, unbothered and eternal. No photograph had ever prepared me for that moment.

The Road to Spiti

Trekking through Spiti Valley is not just a physical journey. The roads are broken, the passes are dangerous, and the weather changes without warning. But that is exactly what makes it unforgettable. You learn very quickly that nature does not negotiate. It does not care about your plans.

The Kunzum Pass sits at 4,590 metres. When you cross it, there is a small temple where travellers stop and pray — not out of religion necessarily, but out of pure, honest humility. You have just driven through clouds. Of course you stop and pray.

Villages Frozen in Time

Kaza, the district headquarters of Spiti, has one ATM, one main road, and the warmest people I have ever met. A family invited me for dinner without knowing my name. We sat on the floor, ate dal and rice, and talked through broken Hindi and hand gestures until midnight.

Ki Monastery stands on a hilltop like it has always been there — because it has, for nearly a thousand years. Monks as young as eight years old walk its corridors with the quiet confidence of people who have figured something out that the rest of us are still searching for.

What the Mountains Teach

If there is one thing the Himalayas teach you, it is patience. The mountains have been here for millions of years. Your deadline, your meeting, your anxiety — none of it matters at 14,000 feet. And somehow, that is the most freeing feeling in the world.

I came back a different person. Not dramatically — I still drink too much coffee and check my phone too often. But somewhere inside, something shifted. A quietness settled in. And on difficult days, I close my eyes and go back to that dhaba, that chai, that mountain — just sitting there, unbothered and eternal.

Should You Go?

Yes. Without question, yes. Go in summer when the roads are open. Carry a warm layer even in July. Learn five words of Hindi and five of Spitian — people will love you for trying. Eat the local food. Sleep in homestays. Turn your phone off for at least one full day.

The Himalayas are not going anywhere. But you — you are always running out of time. So go. Answer the call. The mountains are waiting.
